Editor 'Tom Edison' delivers a lengthy, unusually personal account of TAP's founding and near-destruction: joining publisher Al Bell's staff in 1975, co-editing with Jim Phelps until his 1977 departure, and Al Bell's own resignation in 1977 after which Bell continued handling TAP's mailing labels. The narrative describes Bell withholding labels for months, sending an outdated subscriber tape, and running up unpaid tax and book-club bills in TAP's name, nearly bankrupting the newsletter. TAP recovers by acquiring its own computer system (with help from 'Cheshire' and 'Wizard' to transcribe Bell's subscriber data), severing ties with Bell, and relocating to a new, more secure 'underground' office address.
