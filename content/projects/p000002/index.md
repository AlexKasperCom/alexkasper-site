Through real-world stories spanning cybersecurity, confidence tricks, scams, prompt injection, and AI jailbreaking, this book introduces a framework that names the recurring stages bad-faith actors use to engineer actions before their targets, human or machine, realize what they've done.

Instead of disconnected anecdotes that quickly overwhelm, each example ties back to a single reinforced throughline, so you'll learn to think like an attacker and anticipate how attacks unfold, and in doing so, learn to prevent them.

Whether you're defending an organization, investigating fraud, studying psychology, or simply trying to make better decisions, it offers a new lens for understanding, discussing, and breaking down social engineering attacks.
