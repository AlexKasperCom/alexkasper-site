**Eyeball Eddie** is a 2001 American short dark comedy, produced at the USC School of Cinema-Television and directed by Elizabeth Allen Rosenbaum from a screenplay by Nick Pustay. Alex Kasper (credited as Alexis Kasperavicius) co-produced.{{< fn "n000233" >}}

## Plot

Eddie Malick is a 14-year-old high school wrestler who struggles in matches and feels self-conscious about his prosthetic eye. When the eye accidentally dislodges during a bout, it startles his opponent and hands Eddie an unexpected win. He starts removing it deliberately to distract opponents, and his sudden success draws attention at school and boosts attendance at wrestling meets. His teammate Skelley, initially dismissive, starts hanging around him and takes the prosthetic eye to a party, where it's damaged. After discovering this and fighting Skelley, Eddie's eye shatters entirely. The film ends with Eddie competing without it, wearing a patch instead.

## Cast

* Martin Starr as Eddie
* Michael Rosenbaum as Tom Skelley
* M. Emmet Walsh as Coach Cook{{< fn "n000238" >}}

## Production

Producers on the film included Kasper and Rosenbaum, with Martin Cohen (DreamWorks' head of post-production) and Erica Frauman as executive producers; director Gary Fleder mentored the project. The screenplay was registered with the WGA on January 1, 1999, and the production packet assembled to solicit donor and industry support listed backers including Jack Nicholson, Brett Ratner, Cary Woods, and Scott Rosenberg.{{< fn "n000233" >}}

## Release and Festival Screenings

The film premiered at the Slamdance Film Festival in Park City, Utah, in January 2001,{{< fn "n000237" >}}{{< fn "n000238" >}} then screened at USC's own First Look Festival that April.{{< fn "n000234" >}}{{< fn "n000236" >}} It went on to play the Maryland, Los Angeles, Nashville, Oldenburg International, Sidewalk Moving Picture,{{< fn "n000240" >}} and Stony Brook{{< fn "n000241" >}} film festivals, along with Moomba's Short Film Forum in West Hollywood.

## Reception

Reviewing the film for Film Threat out of Slamdance, Morgan Miller praised M. Emmet Walsh's performance as Eddie's coach, quoting his line to Eddie: "You may have an eye made of glass, but let me tell you something, you've got balls made of brass." Miller's overall verdict was more mixed, calling the film "a clichéd, predictable dark comedy.&rdquo;{{< fn "n000242" >}}

The film picked up several festival honors: the Audience Choice Award for Short Film at the Sidewalk Moving Picture Festival,{{< fn "n000243" >}}{{< fn "n000244" >}} a tie for the Audience Award at the Stony Brook Film Festival,{{< fn "n000245" >}} an Honorable Mention for Best Short Film at the Nashville Film Festival,{{< fn "n000239" >}} and a win at Telluride Indiefest.{{< fn "n000246" >}}
