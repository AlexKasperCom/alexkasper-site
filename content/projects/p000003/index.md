**Return Fire** is a 1995 vehicular combat game developed by Silent Software for the 3DO, the spiritual successor to designer Baron Reichart "Kurt" Von Wolfsheild's earlier 16-bit game Fire Power.{{< fn "n000013" >}} I produced the game and served as its sound designer and music supervisor.{{< fn "n000012" >}}

## Development

Return Fire took about two years to develop, with roughly three months spent on the visual style alone; the 3DO hardware handled between 200,000 and 230,000 polygons per second.{{< fn "n000013" >}} Beyond Von Wolfsheild, the core team included graphic artist Van Arno and programmer Will Ware as well as a good sized team.{{< fn "n000013" >}} Von Wolfsheild described the game as "more a philosophy than a game" built specifically for two-player competition, comparing its design goals to chess, and framed his approach to development as making "Porsches in a VW world.&rdquo;{{< fn "n000013" >}}

Critics repeatedly singled out the game's orchestral soundtrack as central to its identity, describing it as "standout&rdquo;{{< fn "n000032" >}} and central to the tension and spectacle of play.{{< fn "n000024" >}}

## Gameplay

The game is built around a capture-the-flag contest between opposing bases, viewed from a top-down perspective. Players choose among four specialized vehicles, each with distinct strengths and roles: a fragile helicopter that must return to base to resupply, a tank with a rotating turret, the more durable but slower armored support vehicle, and a fast jeep that is the only vehicle capable of carrying the flag but the weakest defensively.{{< fn "n000011" >}} Success depends on scouting the map, matching vehicle to mission, and timing attacks and defenses against mines, drones, and other hazards.{{< fn "n000031" >}}{{< fn "n000035" >}}

## Release

Return Fire was released for the 3DO in 1995.{{< fn "n000012" >}} Ports to the PlayStation and Windows followed in 1996. An expansion pack, *Return Fire: Maps O' Death*, added new maps the same year and was well received in its own right.{{< fn "n000230" >}}

## Reception

Electronic Gaming Monthly named Return Fire its "Game of the Month" and gave it an "Editor's Choice Gold Award," with four reviewers scoring it 9, 9, 7, and 9.{{< fn "n000016" >}} GamePro praised its two-player mode, sound design, and humor.{{< fn "n000025" >}} Edge awarded it 8/10, calling it a deceptively elegant blend of arcade action and strategic cat-and-mouse play.{{< fn "n000024" >}} The Los Angeles Times highlighted its vehicle variety and "gleeful" soundtrack.{{< fn "n000032" >}} Next Generation scored it 4/5, commending the soundtrack and two-player mode.{{< fn "n000226" >}}

The PlayStation port drew similar praise: Next Generation again scored it 4/5,{{< fn "n000227" >}} and GamePro called it "a brilliantly conceived thrill-a-minute war sim," adding that "never before has sound been so instrumental to a game's success.&rdquo;{{< fn "n000231" >}}

## Legacy

Return Fire was named 3DO Two-Player Game of the Year at the 1995 3DO Awards.{{< fn "n000041" >}} In the UK, GamesMaster ranked it #1 on its "3DO Top 10" and #51 on its "Top 100 Games of All Time.&rdquo;{{< fn "n000228" >}} Looking back in 1997, Electronic Gaming Monthly placed it #63 on its list of the 100 best video games of all time, citing its high replay value.{{< fn "n000229" >}}
