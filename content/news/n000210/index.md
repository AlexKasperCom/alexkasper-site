A legal-rights explainer walking through the federal wiretap statute that lets telephone company employees intercept and use communications data when necessary to protect the carrier's own property or service, citing case law (including a Supreme Court-affirmed ruling) establishing that using a phone line for fraud strips away any reasonable expectation of privacy in that call. The bulk of the article explains how phone companies legally use Dialed Number Recorders (DNRs), warrantless devices that log dialed digits and detect specific tones like 2600 Hz associated with blue boxing, framed explicitly as a 'know your rights and risks' piece rather than an evasion guide.
