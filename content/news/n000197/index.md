Summarizes a talk by an FBI agent on the bureau's categorization of computer crime into six tiers of increasing technical sophistication, from simple data alteration by clerks up to penetrating operating systems and telecommunications networks, and cites the era's benchmark cases including Stanley Rifkin's $10 million wire-transfer fraud against Security Pacific Bank. Also reprints a rumor about pen registers being placed on lines suspected of ARPANET dial-up use, with a caution to hackers about phone-hook habits that pen registers can reveal. A companion drug-chemistry column update reports a legal-analogue compound's reclassification to controlled status and advises readers to watch for hidden radio-frequency tracking devices ('beepers') in chemical shipments.
