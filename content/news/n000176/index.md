Presents 'The Programmable Blue Box' by 'Peter Piper,' a keypad-driven tone-dialing circuit built from shift registers, diode-matrix key encoding, and switched-resistor oscillator banks for stability across temperature and voltage. The article walks through the circuit's keyboard debounce logic, its two-bank output-line wiring for the full tone set, and several calibration methods (including a DIP-switch or plug-in wiring scheme) for tuning the oscillators to the correct frequencies. Presented as a hobbyist-grade improvement in reliability over earlier discrete-component box designs.
