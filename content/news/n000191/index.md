Describes an early social-engineering technique for shared-terminal computer systems: writing a program that imitates a mainframe's login prompt closely enough to trick a legitimate user into typing their username and password into it, which the program then logs before handing control back to the real operating system. The piece emphasizes the difficulty of mimicking system error messages and logout behavior convincingly enough to avoid detection. Also offers to answer reader questions about Hewlett-Packard 2000/ACCESS timesharing systems from personal work experience, recommends the novel 'The Adolescence of P-1' about a rogue AI program, and suggests befriending telephone linemen as a way to acquire a lineman's test handset.
